## Video 03 — How to Earn as a Liquidity Provider

When a hedger buys protection on BlockFinaX, someone has to stand behind that payout. That someone is a liquidity provider. In this video, you will learn exactly what that role means, how you earn from it, and what you need to understand before you deposit.

As a liquidity provider, you deposit USDC into a hedge event pool. That deposit backs the payouts that hedgers are entitled to if the strike price is touched. In return, every hedger who buys protection in that event pays a premium — and that premium goes to you.

Think of it this way. The hedger is paying for insurance. You are the one providing it. The premium is your income for taking on that role.

Here is how to get started.

Go to the Hedge marketplace and browse the available events. As a liquidity provider, you are looking at the same events hedgers see, but you are evaluating them differently. You want to assess the strike distance — how far the strike is from the current rate — and the expiry timeline. The further the strike from the current rate, and the shorter the time to expiry, the lower the probability that the event settles with a payout. That is the scenario where you keep all the premiums earned with no payout obligation.

When you find an event you want to back, open it and select the liquidity provider option. Enter the amount of USDC you want to deposit. Confirm the transaction from your wallet. Your deposit is now in the pool and you are earning from that moment.

Now let us talk about pool capacity and utilisation, because this is important to understand.

Every event has a maximum pool size set by the event creator. This cap defines the total payout the pool can support. As hedgers buy positions, they each reserve a portion of that capacity. The amount reserved per hedger is their individual payout — not their premium.

For example, if a hedger's notional is one thousand dollars and their payout is two hundred dollars, the pool reserves two hundred dollars of capacity. That is because the pool only ever needs to pay out two hundred dollars for that position.

Pool utilisation is how much of the total capacity is currently reserved. If utilisation is at sixty percent, forty percent of the pool is still available for new hedgers to enter. If utilisation reaches one hundred percent, the pool is full and no new positions can be opened until an existing position expires or settles.

As a liquidity provider, high utilisation is a positive signal. It means more hedgers are participating, more premiums are being collected, and more of your deposit is actively working.

When the event expires, one of two things happens.

If the strike was never touched, the event closes with no payout. All deposits are returned to liquidity providers in full, plus all premiums collected from hedgers across the life of the event. Your return is purely the accumulated premium income.

If the strike was touched, the oracle settles the event automatically. Hedgers who had active positions receive their payouts from the pool. Your deposit is reduced by the payout obligations your share of the pool covered. The remaining balance, including any premiums already earned, is returned to you.

Your risk as a liquidity provider is that the event settles with a payout. Your reward is the premium income when it does not.

The economics are straightforward. Larger deposits earn proportionally more premiums. Events with strikes far from the current rate carry lower settlement risk. Shorter expiry windows reduce the time the rate has to reach the strike.

You choose the events. You set your deposit size. The protocol handles everything else — tracking positions, calculating payouts, and returning your balance at settlement.

That is how you earn as a liquidity provider on BlockFinaX.

## Video 05 — How Settlement Works

One of the most important things to understand about BlockFinaX is that you never have to manually claim a payout. There is no button to press, no form to submit, no approval process to wait for. Settlement is automatic. This video explains exactly how that works.

Every hedge event on BlockFinaX has a strike price and an expiry date. The strike is the rate level that triggers a payout. The expiry is the deadline by which that level must be reached. Between those two points, the BlockFinaX oracle is watching.

The oracle is a system that runs continuously in the background. Every five minutes, around the clock, it fetches the live exchange rate for every active currency pair on the platform. It compares each rate against the strike prices of all open events in that pair. The moment a rate touches or crosses a strike, settlement is triggered.

This is called a one-touch mechanic. The rate only needs to touch the strike once — at any point before expiry — for the payout to be triggered. It does not need to stay there. It does not need to close above or below the strike. One touch is enough.

When the oracle detects a strike has been touched, it calls the settlement function on the blockchain immediately. The smart contract calculates each hedger's payout based on their notional amount and the distance between the initial rate and the strike price. Those payouts are sent directly from the pool to each hedger's wallet in the same transaction.

The payout each hedger receives is fixed and predetermined. It was calculated at the moment they bought their position and has not changed since. There is no ambiguity, no renegotiation, and no discretion involved. The contract executes exactly what was agreed at entry.

For liquidity providers, the same settlement event returns their remaining balance. Their deposit, minus the payout obligations their share of the pool covered, plus any premiums accumulated during the life of the event, is returned to their wallet automatically.

If the rate never touches the strike before the expiry date and time, the event closes at expiry. No payouts are made to hedgers. All liquidity provider deposits are returned in full, plus all premiums collected from hedgers across the event's lifetime. Hedgers lose only the premium they paid when they entered.

Every step of this process happens on the Lisk blockchain. The oracle's rate checks, the settlement call, the payout transfers — all of it is recorded on-chain and publicly verifiable. Anyone can inspect the transaction, the rate at the time of settlement, and the amounts transferred. Nothing is handled off-chain or behind closed doors.

To summarise. You buy protection. The oracle watches the rate. The rate touches the strike. You get paid. Automatically, immediately, and exactly the amount that was agreed when you entered.

That is how settlement works on BlockFinaX.

## Video 07 — Understanding Your Premium

When you buy protection on BlockFinaX, you pay a premium. This video explains what the premium is, how it is calculated, and how to think about it before you commit to a position.

The premium is the cost of your protection. It is the maximum amount you can lose on any single hedge position. If the rate never touches the strike before expiry, you lose only the premium. If the rate does touch the strike, you receive your payout and the premium is effectively recovered as part of that outcome.

Think of it like paying for coverage in advance. You know the cost upfront. You know the potential payout upfront. Nothing changes between the moment you enter and the moment the event closes.

The premium is calculated as a percentage of your notional amount. That percentage — the premium rate — is set by the event creator and is visible on every event card in the marketplace. A premium rate of two percent on a notional of one thousand dollars means you pay twenty dollars for protection on that position. Your payout, if triggered, would be based on the distance between the initial rate and the strike price applied to your notional.

Two factors primarily determine how much a premium rate reflects the risk of the event.

The first is strike distance. The closer the strike is to the current rate, the more likely it is to be touched before expiry. Events with strikes close to the current rate carry higher inherent risk for the pool, and creators tend to price those with higher premium rates. Events with strikes further away are less likely to trigger, so premium rates are typically lower.

The second is time to expiry. More time gives the rate more opportunity to reach the strike. A thirty-day event carries more risk than a three-day event at the same strike distance. Longer events generally command higher premiums to compensate for that additional exposure.

When you are browsing events, compare premium rates and payout sizes across similar pairs and directions. An event with a lower premium rate but a reasonable payout might represent better value than one priced aggressively close to the current rate.

The premium is not a fee that disappears into the platform. It is distributed to the liquidity providers who backed the pool and to the event creator as a loyalty fee. Everyone who contributed to making that event available earns from the premium you pay.

Your decision as a hedger is simple. Is the cost of this premium worth the protection it provides given your actual exposure? If the answer is yes, enter the position. If not, wait for an event that fits better.

## Video 09 — Risks Every User Should Know

Starting with hedgers.

As a hedger, your maximum loss is the premium you pay when you enter a position. That is fixed. If the rate never touches the strike before expiry, the event closes and your premium is not returned. For event creators.

For all users.

Rate data comes from external oracle sources. While the system is designed to be reliable, extreme market conditions or data provider outages could affect rate updates. Understanding these risks does not make BlockFinaX less useful. It makes you a more prepared and effective user. Manage your position sizes, diversify your exposure, and use the platform for what it was built for — hedging real risk, not maximising speculative return.

## Video 10 — What is a Currency Pair?

Before you can use the BlockFinaX hedge marketplace, you need to understand one foundational concept — the currency pair. This video explains what a currency pair is and how to read it.

A currency pair is simply the price of one currency expressed in terms of another. When you see USD slash GHS, that means US Dollar slash Ghana Cedi. The number next to it — for example, 15.40 — means one US Dollar currently buys 15.40 Ghana Cedis.

In every currency pair, there are two currencies. The one on the left is called the base currency. The one on the right is called the quote currency. The rate tells you how many units of the quote currency are needed to buy one unit of the base currency.

So in USD slash GHS at 15.40, the dollar is the base and the cedi is the quote. The rate is 15.40 cedis per dollar.

When the rate goes up — say from 15.40 to 16.00 — it means the dollar has strengthened and the cedi has weakened. You now need more cedis to buy one dollar. For a Ghanaian importer paying suppliers in dollars, this is bad news. Their costs just went up in local currency terms.

When the rate goes down — say from 15.40 to 14.80 — it means the dollar has weakened and the cedi has strengthened. For the same importer, this is good news. Their dollar costs now convert into fewer cedis.

On BlockFinaX, every hedge event is tied to a currency pair. When you see a downward event on USD slash GHS, it means the event pays out if the rate falls — if the dollar weakens against the cedi. When you see an upward event on the same pair, it means the event pays out if the rate rises — if the dollar strengthens against the cedi.

Choosing the right event always starts with understanding which direction a rate move would hurt you. If a rising rate hurts you, you want an upward event. If a falling rate hurts you, you want a downward event.

BlockFinaX covers over forty African currency pairs alongside metals, energy, agricultural commodities, and crypto. The pair you need almost certainly exists on the platform.

Understanding the pair is the first step to using it.

## Video 11 — What is USDC and Why Does BlockFinaX Use It?

All transactions on BlockFinaX — premiums, payouts, liquidity deposits — are conducted in USDC. If you are new to blockchain finance, you may be wondering what USDC is and why it is used instead of a local currency. This video explains.

USDC is a stablecoin. A stablecoin is a digital currency whose value is designed to stay fixed to a reference asset. In the case of USDC, that reference asset is the US Dollar. One USDC is always worth one US Dollar. It does not fluctuate the way Bitcoin or Ethereum does.

USDC is issued by Circle, a regulated financial company in the United States. Every USDC in circulation is backed by an equivalent amount of US Dollar reserves held in regulated financial institutions. The reserves are audited and publicly reported. You can verify the backing at any time.

Because USDC lives on a blockchain, it can be sent anywhere in the world in seconds, at any time, without going through a bank. There are no clearing windows, no correspondent banking fees, and no geographic restrictions. If you have a wallet address and USDC, you can transact.

BlockFinaX uses USDC for three reasons.

First, stability. If premiums and payouts were denominated in a volatile cryptocurrency, the value of your protection would be uncertain. Using USDC means a ten-dollar premium is always ten dollars, and a two-hundred-dollar payout is always two hundred dollars. The hedge product needs a stable unit of account to work as intended.

Second, accessibility. USDC is available globally and can be acquired through a wide range of exchanges and on-ramp services. Users across Africa can obtain USDC through local crypto exchanges or peer-to-peer platforms and bring it directly into BlockFinaX.

Third, composability. Because USDC operates on blockchain infrastructure, it integrates naturally with smart contracts. Payouts can be sent automatically and instantly without any manual banking process in between.

To use BlockFinaX, you need USDC in your wallet. Once it is there, the platform takes care of everything else — premiums, payouts, pool deposits, and fee distributions — all in USDC, all on-chain, all automatic.
